Why will you not just tell me a price over the phone?

Because nobody honestly can. A real premium comes from a completed application: your payroll, your class codes, your history, reviewed by a carrier underwriter. Anyone who states a number before that is guessing, and the guess gets corrected later at your expense.

What we do instead is show you the published rate components and filings that will shape your price, each with a source you can open, and then help you apply so the carrier can produce the real number. The mechanics are laid out in how workers comp pricing works in New York.

What is the written findings package?

Before we ask you for anything, we research public records for your shop: state filings, county licence registries, and the rate bulletins that apply to your trade. The result is a short written document specific to your business, with the math shown and a citation next to every figure.

Anything we cannot know from public data is stated plainly as unknown. You can read it, open every source yourself, and stop right there if you want. There is no obligation attached to reading.

What happens after I submit the application?

The ten-field application at /apply.html starts the carrier paperwork. We prepare the forms from what you send, you review every page and e-sign it personally, and the carrier makes the decision. Nothing takes effect on our say-so; approval always belongs to the carrier.

If the carrier approves, your first payment and every payment after it goes directly to the carrier, never to this office.

How long does workers comp take to set up?

Two very different answers. A brand-new business with no prior workers comp history can e-apply, e-sign, and pay a premium deposit electronically, and coverage can take effect at 12:01 a.m. the following day if the carrier approves. That approval always belongs to the carrier.

An established shop switching carriers typically waits 5 to 25 days, and most of that is waiting on loss runs (typically five years) from the current carrier. The full timeline for each path is on the workers comp page, and new operations should read the new contractor guide.

Money and payments

Where the money goes, and why none of it comes here.

How does a producer get paid? Do I owe you a fee?

You never pay us a fee. The carrier pays a commission out of the premium it charges, which is the standard way licensed producers are compensated in New York. There is no invoice from this office, ever.

The premium itself comes from the carrier's filed rates and your application, and working with this office adds nothing on top of it.

Why does my premium go to the carrier and never to you?

Because money handling is where trouble starts in this industry, so we designed it out. Your payment goes straight to the insurance company that issues your policy, you get that carrier's own receipt, and there is never a question about where the funds sat in between.

We are paid by commission from the carrier, so there is no reason for your money to touch this office, and it never does.

What actually shapes the price of a workers comp policy?

Four things do most of the work: the class codes assigned to your operations, each with a published loss cost per $100 of payroll; your payroll itself; your own history, once you have one; and the carrier's programs and credits at underwriting.

The baseline moves too: the New York statewide loss cost level drops 21.9% effective October 1, 2026 (NYCIRB Bulletin 2659). None of this is a quote; it is the published arithmetic a quote gets built from. The long version is in how workers comp pricing works.

Why do you publish the math?

Because in a market this small, being confidently wrong ends a reputation, and being vague earns nothing. Every figure on this site links to the public filing it came from, so you can check our work before you ever talk to us.

Our internal rule is simple: if a finding cannot be defended from first principles on a phone call, it does not go in writing. The about page explains the method.

What is a payroll audit, and why can the final premium differ from the deposit?

A workers comp premium starts as an estimate built on the payroll you project for the year. When the policy year ends, the carrier audits your actual payroll. If actual payroll came in higher than projected, the carrier charges the difference; if lower, money comes back. That is not a trick; it is how the product is designed.

We help you project realistically at the start and prepare the records the auditor will ask for, and we will never promise where an audit lands. The workers comp page covers what the auditor looks at.

Workers comp specifics

Class codes, safety groups, subs, owners, and the October 2026 change.

Is workers comp really required from my very first employee?

Yes. New York requires workers compensation for virtually every employer from the first employee, under the New York Workers Compensation Law. It is not tied to a headcount threshold the way some other requirements are.

Operating without it invites state penalties on top of the exposure itself, so treat it as day-one paperwork, not something to grow into. Start with the workers comp page.

What are class codes?

A class code is how the rating system describes the work your people do. Each code carries a published loss cost per $100 of payroll, which is the raw material a carrier turns into a premium. Plumbing, for example, is class 5183, and its loss cost goes from 4.365 to 3.519 per $100 of payroll on October 1, 2026, down 19.4% (NYCIRB Bulletin 2659).

Getting the code right matters in both directions: the wrong code can mean paying more than the work calls for, or an unpleasant correction at audit. Classification is one of the first things we check in the findings package.

What are safety groups, and what is a dividend?

NYSIF safety groups pool similar businesses so they can be priced on the group's record. The published upfront discounts average 32.5%, and the groups have historically declared annual dividends averaging around 30%. Dividends are declared each year after the fact and are never assured; treat them as history, not a promise.

Admission is not automatic: it requires the group manager's acceptance and NYSIF underwriting. Whether a group fits your shop is exactly the kind of question the findings package answers. There is a Long Island deep dive at safety groups on Long Island.

I heard workers comp rates are dropping. Is that true?

The published baseline is. NYCIRB Bulletin 2659 sets the New York statewide loss cost level 21.9% lower effective October 1, 2026. A loss cost is a component of a premium, not the premium itself, so your own figure still depends on your application and the carrier.

What it does mean: if your renewal falls after that date, the arithmetic underneath your price is changing, and it is worth a fifteen-minute look. The full breakdown is at the October 2026 rate change, explained.

Do my 1099 subcontractors need to be counted?

It depends on the facts, and anyone who gives you a one-word answer is skipping the hard part. New York looks at the substance of the relationship, not the label on the tax form: who directs the work, who supplies materials and tools, and whether the sub genuinely runs an independent business with its own insurance. The Workers Compensation Board publishes guidance on the distinction.

Bring your subs' certificates of insurance to the conversation. Collecting them and keeping them current is the single best habit here, and it is also what a payroll auditor will ask to see.

Can owners exclude themselves from workers comp?

Sometimes, and the rules depend on your entity type. Sole proprietors, partners, corporate officers, and LLC members are each treated differently under New York law, and an election that fits one shop is wrong for the next one.

The decision has real consequences if an owner is hurt on a job, so we walk through the options for your specific entity and point you to the official Workers Compensation Board guidance. We do not give legal advice, and this is a spot where a quick call beats a form.

What about jobs outside New York?

Every state runs its own workers comp system, so a policy set up for New York work needs the right state listings before your crew crosses a state line. If you take jobs in Connecticut, New Jersey, or anywhere else, say so on the application.

Structuring it correctly up front is cheap. Finding the gap after an out-of-state injury is not. This is a standard item in our application review, so just tell us where you actually work.

What is DBL, and how is it different from workers comp?

They respond to different events. Workers compensation responds to injury and illness that arise from the job. New York DBL, the statutory short-term disability benefit, responds when an employee is disabled by something off the job. Both are mandatory for private New York employers; the state's employer overview covers the DBL side.

DBL is usually handled alongside the rest of your benefits, and it is one of the items on the employee benefits page.

What is PFL?

Paid Family Leave is New York's mandatory program giving employees paid time away from work to bond with a new child or care for a family member with a serious health condition. Like DBL, it is required of private New York employers; the state's employer overview explains the employer obligations.

PFL and DBL typically ride together administratively, which is why we handle them as part of employee benefits rather than as an afterthought.

Policies and paperwork

Certificates, claims, and switching.

What is a certificate of insurance, and how fast can I get one?

A certificate of insurance is a one-page summary showing the policies you carry, their limits, and their dates. General contractors and property owners ask for one before you get on the job, and some will not schedule you without it.

Once your policy is issued, a standard certificate is routine service, usually a same-day request during business hours. One honest caveat: a certificate summarizes a policy, it does not change one, so if a GC asks for terms the policy does not include, that is a conversation with the carrier, not a wording fix on the certificate. More on the general liability page.

What happens when there is a claim?

Two calls: the carrier's claim line, and us. The carrier runs the claim; that is its contract and its adjusters. Our job is to make sure the first report is complete, the deadlines are met, and you understand what happens at each step, and we stay in it until it is resolved.

Report early. Late reporting is the most common self-inflicted wound in claims, and it is entirely avoidable.

Should I switch mid-term or wait for renewal?

Both happen, and the right answer is arithmetic, not preference. Leaving mid-term can trigger a short-rate cancellation charge under many policies' terms, and it means re-doing paperwork now. Moving at renewal is cleaner and lines up with the annual audit cycle.

Which one wins depends on your dates and the size of what you would gain by moving. That comparison is a standard part of the fifteen-minute check, and we will tell you plainly if the honest answer is to stay put until renewal.

I have workers comp. Do I still need general liability?

They answer different problems. Workers comp responds when your own employee is injured on the job. General liability responds when your work injures someone else or damages someone else's property. One does not substitute for the other, and most GCs require both before you set foot on site.

The full comparison is at general liability vs workers comp, and vehicles are their own line again: see commercial auto.
