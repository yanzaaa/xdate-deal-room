The story, honestly

This practice is one licensed producer, Anthony Yanza, working from an office on West Suffolk Avenue in Central Islip. What makes it unusual is the tooling: Anthony builds the research software this practice runs on. It reads public sources, New York rate filings, state program pages, and county contractor records, and turns them into findings a shop owner can check line by line.

The pitch is simple. Most agencies quote first and explain never: you hand over your payroll, a number comes back, and the reasoning stays in a black box. This practice runs the other direction. Research from public filings comes first, a written findings package comes second, and only then a short application you review and e-sign yourself. When we say the statewide workers comp loss cost level falls 21.9% on October 1, 2026, the citation is NYCIRB Bulletin 2659, and the plain-English version is on the blog.

Public data has limits, and the practice is built to respect them. We work from filings and public records, never from your policy, so findings arrive as likelihoods about most shops in your class, each with its source attached. And no premium ever appears on this site or in any package, because an honest premium requires your completed application and a decision that belongs to the carrier alone. That is the standard everything below is held to.

Our rules

Five working rules, enforced on every account. They are the difference between this practice and a quote machine, and you can hold us to each one.

Never a premium without your application

No dollar figure on this site or in any findings package is a premium. A real number exists only after you complete the application and the carrier prices it. Anyone who quotes your shop sight unseen is guessing.

Every figure cited to a public source

Loss costs, discount averages, effective dates: each links to the filing or program page it came from, so you can check the math without taking our word for it. The pricing explainer shows the whole method.

Premiums go to the carrier, never to us

You never pay this office a fee. Premium is paid to the insurance carrier directly, and the carrier pays the producer a commission. That is the entire money flow, end to end.

You e-sign everything yourself

Nothing is submitted in your name without your review. You read the application, the terms, and every figure, then e-sign personally. No signatures on your behalf, ever.

Your numbers come from you

Payroll, revenue, and loss history go on an application exactly as you state them. We never estimate them for you, because a wrong figure on an insurance application is a serious problem, and it is your name on it.

What working with us looks like

Seven steps, start to renewal. The same process applies to workers comp, general liability, commercial auto, and employee benefits, and you can stop at any step.

  1. 1

    Research

    We study public filings and records for your trade and county: rate bulletins, state program pages, contractor records. This happens before any contact, with no forms and no obligation.

  2. 2

    Written findings package

    You get a short written package: what the public record suggests about most shops in your class, the math behind each finding, and the source for every figure.

  3. 3

    Fifteen-minute check

    One phone call to test the findings against reality. You correct what public data cannot show, and anything that does not survive the conversation gets crossed out.

  4. 4

    Ten-field application

    If the findings hold up, a ten-field online application starts the carrier paperwork. You supply your own payroll and history; we never fill in a number for you.

  5. 5

    You e-sign

    You review the completed application and the final terms, then e-sign personally. Nothing goes to a carrier without your signature on it.

  6. 6

    The carrier decides

    Underwriting and approval belong to the carrier alone. If the carrier approves, coverage can take effect on the date the policy states. New employers sometimes move faster than shops switching carriers.

  7. 7

    Service and renewal reviews

    After a policy is issued: certificates, changes, and questions, plus a renewal review each year that rechecks the current filings, including rate changes like the one effective October 1, 2026.
