Workers comp, explained

NYSIF safety groups, explained for Long Island contractors.

Trade employers across New York pool their workers comp inside the State Insurance Fund through safety groups, and the numbers behind them are some of the most public math in commercial insurance. Here is what a group is, what the published discounts and dividends actually mean, and how admission is really decided.

Published July 25, 2026 · 7 minute read · Anthony Yanza, NY licensed Property and Casualty producer, licence no. 1986535

What a safety group actually is

The New York State Insurance Fund (NYSIF) writes workers compensation across the state. A safety group is a pool of employers in the same trade, insured through NYSIF and managed by a group administrator, usually called the group manager. Plumbers pool with plumbers, electricians with electricians, and so on down the trade list.

The idea is old and simple: a trade that polices its own safety earns better collective results than the market at large, and the members share in those results. The group manager runs admission, sets the group's safety expectations, and handles the paperwork between the members and NYSIF. You are still insured by NYSIF; the group is the room you sit in.

One framing note before the numbers. NYSIF is a state fund, and references to it here are educational. Nothing on this page implies an appointment or affiliation, and every admission decision belongs to the group manager and NYSIF underwriting, never to us.

The published economics, with the source

NYSIF publishes the plan economics itself, which is rare in this industry. Two figures matter. Upfront discounts across safety groups average 32.5%, applied at the start of the policy year. On top of that, groups have historically declared annual dividends averaging around 30%, and that second number comes with a large asterisk we will get to in a moment.

32.5%Average published upfront discount across NYSIF safety groups
~30%Historical annual dividends on average, never assured, declared yearly
21.9%Statewide loss cost level drop effective October 1, 2026 (NYCIRB Bulletin 2659)

Two cautions before you do envelope math. First, the dividend is retrospective. It is declared yearly, after the policy year closes, based on how the group actually performed. It is not a promise, it is not on your invoice at the start of the year, and in a bad year for the group it can be smaller than the historical average or nothing at all. Second, neither figure is a quote. Your own number depends on your class codes, your payroll, and a completed application, and the decision always belongs to the carrier.

These group economics also sit on top of a falling baseline. The statewide loss cost level drops 21.9% effective October 1, 2026 under NYCIRB Bulletin 2659, which we wrote up in the October 2026 rate change post. If you want the full chain from published loss cost to the figure on your policy, start with how workers comp pricing works in New York.

How admission actually works

Admission is paperwork, not luck. Two doors have to open, in order, and no producer can open either one for you by charm alone.

  1. 1

    Find the group for your trade

    Safety groups are organized by trade. Step one is confirming a group exists for your classification and is accepting members. This is research, and it costs you nothing.

  2. 2

    The group manager reviews you

    The manager protects the whole group’s results, so they look at your operations, loss history, and safety practices before accepting you. Acceptance is theirs to give.

  3. 3

    NYSIF underwrites the application

    NYSIF reviews the application like any insurer. The group manager’s acceptance plus NYSIF’s approval together admit you. Neither one alone is enough.

That is the whole mechanism. There is no lottery and no secret handshake, just two reviews that reward shops who arrive with their records in order.

Who tends to get in

We work from public information, never from your file, so what follows is a tendency, not a statement about your shop. The pattern is consistent, though: groups exist to keep collective losses low, so shops with an established safety record tend to have the easiest conversations. A few clean years of loss history, payroll records that line up with the class codes, and a safety program that exists on paper and in practice are what a group manager most wants to see.

A brand-new shop with no loss history is a different conversation, not automatically a closed door, and the workers comp path for new businesses is different in general. If that is you, read our guide for brand-new contractors first.

What to bring to the conversation

If you decide to explore a group, walk in with the file already built:

  • Loss runs. Five years from your current insurer, if you have prior history. This is usually the slow part: an established shop switching insurers typically waits 5 to 25 days, and most of that is waiting on the incumbent to produce loss runs.
  • Payroll records by class code. Workers comp pricing runs on payroll, so clean records sorted by classification make every later step faster.
  • A plain description of operations. What work you do, where, and how much of it you subcontract out.
  • Your safety program, in writing. Training records, toolbox talks, incident procedures. The group manager's whole job is believing this document.

The honest trade-offs

Safety groups are a real mechanism with real published numbers, and they are still not for everyone. Three things deserve saying plainly.

The dividend is not income you can budget. It is declared yearly, in arrears, from group results you do not control alone. If your cash flow only works when the historical average shows up, the plan is wrong, not the year.

Membership has rules. Groups hold members to safety standards and reporting expectations, because one careless member drags down everyone's results. If you want no one looking over your safety program, a group is a poor fit.

Admission is never yours to assume. Group manager acceptance plus NYSIF underwriting means two independent reviews, and nobody can promise the outcome of either one up front. Anyone who does promise it is telling you something about themselves.

Nothing on this page is a quote or a promise of admission. The discount and dividend figures are NYSIF's published numbers for its safety group plans as a whole, current as of the date above. Your own figure depends on your application, and every admission and pricing decision belongs to the group manager and to NYSIF.
