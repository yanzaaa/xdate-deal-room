Who must carry it

Under the New York Workers Compensation Law, virtually every employer in the state must carry workers compensation from the first employee. Part-time counts. Family members on payroll generally count. Calling a worker a 1099 subcontractor does not settle the question by itself; the Workers Compensation Board looks at the actual working relationship, and on Long Island job sites that question comes up constantly.

If you have no employees at all, the requirement can look different, and the details depend on your entity type and your contracts. Many general contractors ask every sub for a certificate regardless of what the law requires, so plenty of one-person shops end up carrying a policy anyway. If you are not sure where you land, ask us or check the WCB directly. More edge cases are in our questions and answers.

Two related obligations ride alongside workers comp for private New York employers: statutory short-term disability (DBL) and Paid Family Leave (PFL). Both are mandatory and both are separate policies. The WCB employer overview covers the rules, and our employee benefits page covers how shops usually handle them.

What the policy actually pays for

Workers compensation is a no-fault system: when an employee is hurt on the job, the policy responds without a lawsuit deciding who was careless.

Part one

Medical care

Treatment for a work injury or illness, from the ER visit through follow-up care, handled through the workers comp system rather than the employee's own health plan.

Part one

Lost wages

Partial wage replacement while an injured employee cannot work, paid on the schedule New York law sets. For a small crew, this is the piece that keeps one bad day from becoming a payroll crisis.

Part two

Employer liability protection

Defense and protection for the business itself in the narrower situations where an injury claim lands outside the statutory benefit system and comes at the employer directly.

Know the line

What it is not

Workers comp responds for your own people. Damage or injury to third parties, a customer, a neighbor, another trade, is the job of general liability. The two are constantly confused; we wrote up the difference in GL vs workers comp.

How class codes work

Workers comp pricing in New York starts with a classification system. Every kind of work is assigned a class code, and every class code carries a published loss cost: a rate per $100 of payroll that reflects how often, and how badly, people in that work get hurt statewide. Roofers carry a different number than office staff, and plumbers carry their own.

Class 5183 is the New York class for plumbing work, and it is the code most Long Island plumbing and heating shops will recognize from their own paperwork. Its published loss cost is the starting point of the math: multiply your payroll (in hundreds) by the loss cost, then the carrier applies its own approved factors, your experience history, and any credits or programs. The final figure requires a completed application, which is why nobody can honestly state your premium from a website.

The full chain from class code to final number, step by step, is in how workers comp pricing works in New York.

The October 2026 loss cost drop

NYCIRB Bulletin 2659 sets the New York statewide loss cost level 21.9% lower effective October 1, 2026. For class 5183, the published loss cost falls from 4.365 to 3.519 per $100 of payroll, down 19.4%.

A loss cost is not a premium; it is the published baseline your price is built on. But when the baseline moves 19.4% for a class like 5183, the date your policy renews starts to matter, and it is worth knowing where your renewal falls relative to October 1. We wrote a plain-English walkthrough of the filing, what it does and does not mean for a Long Island shop, in the October 2026 rate drop, explained.

Safety groups, explained honestly

NYSIF, the state insurance fund, runs trade-based safety groups. Two levers make them interesting, and one honest caveat keeps the picture straight.

The upfront discount is published and applies at the start. The dividend is a look-back payment declared each year based on the group's actual results; NYSIF reports historical annual dividends averaging around 30%, and history is all it is. No dividend is ever assured, and treating one as a sure thing is the classic safety-group mistake. The published figures are on the NYSIF safety group page.

The other honest caveat: admission is not automatic. The group manager has to accept the shop and NYSIF underwriting has to approve it, so a clean safety record does the talking. Whether a group fits your shop, and which trade groups operate near Nassau and Suffolk, is the subject of NYSIF safety groups for Long Island contractors. We raise the option where the research supports it; we are not the decision-maker, and we do not speak for NYSIF.

How fast it can start

The honest answer depends on one thing: whether there is a prior policy whose records have to be collected.

A brand-new business with no prior workers comp history can e-apply, e-sign, and pay a premium deposit electronically on day 0, and coverage can take effect at 12:01 a.m. the following day if the carrier approves the application. That approval always belongs to the carrier, but for a shop hiring its first employee this is one of the fastest doors in commercial insurance. The full playbook is in workers comp for the new contractor.

An established shop switching carriers typically waits 5 to 25 days, and most of that wait is one document: loss runs (typically five years), which only the incumbent carrier can produce. The request usually goes out in the first week; how quickly it comes back is out of everyone's hands but theirs. Plan a switch around your renewal date, not against a deadline.

Workers comp questions, answered

The short versions. Longer answers, and everything beyond workers comp, live in the full questions and answers.

Do I need workers comp with just one part-time employee?

In New York, almost certainly yes. The requirement applies to virtually every employer from the first employee, and part-time employees count. The Workers Compensation Board is the authority on the edge cases.

I work alone with no employees. Do I still need a policy?

The legal requirement is tied to having employees, and the answer for a true one-person shop depends on entity type and circumstances, so check the WCB or ask us. The practical reality on Long Island is separate: many GCs require a certificate from every sub before anyone walks on the job, so plenty of solo operators carry a policy for the work it wins them.

What will my workers comp cost?

Nobody can state your premium without a completed application, and you should be suspicious of anyone who tries. What is public: your class code's loss cost (for class 5183 plumbing, 3.519 per $100 of payroll from October 1, 2026, per NYCIRB Bulletin 2659), which is then shaped by your payroll, your history, and the carrier's approved factors. The full chain is in how pricing works.

How fast can coverage start?

A brand-new business with no prior workers comp can e-apply and e-sign electronically, and coverage can take effect at 12:01 a.m. the next day if the carrier approves. A shop switching carriers typically waits 5 to 25 days, mostly on five years of loss runs from the incumbent. Details in the new-contractor playbook.

Does workers comp include DBL and PFL?

No. New York statutory short-term disability (DBL) and Paid Family Leave (PFL) are mandatory for private employers and are written separately. The WCB employer overview has the rules; our benefits page covers how to handle them alongside group coverage.
